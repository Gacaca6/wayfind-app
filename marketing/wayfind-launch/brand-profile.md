# Wayfind — campaign brand profile
Version 1 · 18 October 2026 · English campaign

## Identity and position
Wayfind helps people find Scripture for what they feel, read the Bible, and encourage one another in Fellowship. Its own tagline is “Scripture for the searching heart.” The campaign connects a private moment of reading to a familiar act of sharing.

## Audience
Primary: Christians in Rwanda who use their phones for Bible reading and everyday conversations. They want Kinyarwanda reading and a simple path from a feeling to a relevant passage.
Secondary: Bible-study groups and prayer communities that want an approachable place to read and talk together.

## Voice
Warm, clear, hopeful. Short invitations, concrete features, little punctuation. Say “read,” “share a verse,” “encourage one another,” and “pray together.” Avoid “revolutionary,” “life-changing,” guilt about devotion, guaranteed spiritual outcomes, medical claims, or invented popularity.

## Evidence
The deployed app demonstrates feeling-based search, NIV (default), KJV and Kinyarwanda Bible reading, daily verses, community rooms, member-only conversations, and verse/photo attachments. No ratings, endorsements, testimonials or user-count claims are available for marketing.

## Offer and CTA
Open https://app.wayfind.workers.dev. Start by choosing a feeling or reading today's verse. Do not use App Store or Google Play download badges: this campaign promotes the web app.

## Visual identity
Use the existing Wayfind logo, DM Sans and Lora. Cream #FAF7F2, dark text #2C1A0E, brown #5C3D2E and amber #C8873A. Keep Fellowship green inside genuine UI. See source/composition/frame.md for the video specification.

## Operational defaults
English copy; Kinyarwanda text may appear inside authentic product screenshots to demonstrate that feature. No generated people, voice clones or avatars. No voiceover. Use Brag's bundled music and CC0 effects with provenance. Add alt text to delivered graphics. Keep essential video copy visible and readable without sound. Use only staged or anonymous screens; no credentials, real customer chats or personal information.

## Examples and limits
Existing product copy to emulate: “How are you feeling?”; “A verse worth sharing”; “Pray with one another”; “Scripture for the searching heart.” No example social posts or existing campaign results were supplied; these are product-language references, not invented historical posts.
